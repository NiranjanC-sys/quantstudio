T0 is skippable, and this is where you skip it. Eight probes, one per module,
each chosen because failing it predicts failing something specific later. The
diagnostic is not scored as a single number: each probe writes a mastery
estimate to one node of your prerequisite graph, and the scheduler then puts the
unmastered nodes in front of you and hides the rest.

Work them on paper before opening the answers. A probe you answer by
recognising the answer rather than deriving it should be counted wrong, because
the graph is trying to model what you can do, not what looks familiar.

## Probe 1: probabilistic dynamics and entropy

Node: **t0.m1**. Failing this predicts trouble with the Born rule in T1.M2 and
with sampling error in T4.

<Check question="A bit flips 0 to 1 with probability $0.2$ and 1 to 0 with probability $0.5$. Write the operator, apply it twice to the certain state $\ket{0}$, give the stationary distribution, and give its entropy in bits.">
Columns are conditional distributions, so

$$
M = \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & 0.5 \\ 0.2 & 0.5 \end{pmatrix}.
$$

One step: $(0.8, 0.2)$. Two steps: $(0.64 + 0.10,\; 0.16 + 0.10) = (0.74, 0.26)$.

With $a = 0.2$ and $b = 0.5$, the stationary distribution is
$\pi = (b, a)/(a+b) = (0.5, 0.2)/0.7 = (0.714286,\; 0.285714)$. Check it:
$0.8 \times 0.714286 + 0.5 \times 0.285714 = 0.714286$.

Its entropy is $H(\pi) = 0.863121$ bits, below the maximum of 1 because the
distribution is biased.
</Check>

## Probe 2: complex numbers in polar form

Node: **t0.m2**. Failing this predicts trouble with phase kickback in T1.M9 and
with the Fourier transform in T2.

<Check question="Write $z = 1 - i\sqrt3$ in the form $re^{i\theta}$ with the principal argument, then compute $z^3$.">
$|z| = \sqrt{1 + 3} = 2$, and the point is in the fourth quadrant with
$\tan\theta = -\sqrt3$, so $\theta = -\pi/3$ and $z = 2e^{-i\pi/3}$.

By de Moivre, $z^3 = 8 e^{-i\pi} = -8$. The answer is a real number, which is
the check: cubing an angle of $-60$ degrees lands on $-180$ degrees, on the
negative real axis.
</Check>

## Probe 3: inner products in the complex case

Node: **t0.m3**. Failing this predicts trouble with measurement in a rotated
basis, which is all of T1.M2.

<Check question="For $a = (2i,\; 1)^{T}$ and $b = (1,\; -i)^{T}$ in $\mathbb{C}^2$, compute $\langle a, b\rangle$, $\lVert a \rVert$ and $\lVert b\rVert$, and check Cauchy-Schwarz.">
Conjugate the left vector: $\bar a = (-2i,\; 1)$. Then

$$
\langle a, b\rangle = (-2i)(1) + (1)(-i) = -2i - i = -3i .
$$

Norms: $\lVert a\rVert = \sqrt{4 + 1} = \sqrt5 = 2.236068$ and
$\lVert b \rVert = \sqrt{1+1} = \sqrt2 = 1.414214$.

Cauchy-Schwarz: $|-3i| = 3$ against $\sqrt5\sqrt2 = \sqrt{10} = 3.162278$. The
inequality holds and is nearly tight, so the two vectors are close to parallel.
</Check>

## Probe 4: spectra and exponentials

Node: **t0.m4**. Failing this predicts trouble with rotation gates in T1.M3 and
with Hamiltonian simulation in T4.

<Check question="Find the eigenvalues of $A = \big(\begin{smallmatrix} 3 & -i \\ i & 3\end{smallmatrix}\big)$, and state $e^{-i\theta Y}$ in closed form.">
$A = 3I + Y$, and $Y$ has eigenvalues $\pm 1$, so $A$ has eigenvalues $4$ and
$2$. Both are real, as Hermiticity requires. By characteristic polynomial:
$(3-\lambda)^2 - (-i)(i) = (3-\lambda)^2 - 1$, with roots $\lambda = 4, 2$.

Since $Y^2 = I$, the Pauli exponential formula applies:

$$
e^{-i\theta Y} = \cos\theta\, I - i \sin\theta\, Y .
$$

The rotation gate is the half-angle version, $R_y(\varphi) = e^{-i\varphi Y/2}$.
</Check>

## Probe 5: tensor products and ordering

Node: **t0.m5**. Failing this is the single best predictor of a wrong answer
that looks right, because endianness bugs are silent.

<Check question="A four-qubit basis state has only qubit 2 set. Give the RedQuant label and index, and the Qiskit label and index.">
The assignment is $(q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3) = (0,0,1,0)$.

RedQuant: qubit 0 is the most significant bit, so the label is $0010$ and the
index is $2$.

Qiskit: qubit $q$ carries weight $2^{q}$, so the index is $4$, and the printed
label puts qubit 3 leftmost, giving $0100$.

The two indices are $2$ and $4$, which are 4-bit reversals of each other.
</Check>

## Probe 6: reading a figure

Node: **t0.m5** again, this time on the amplitudes rather than the labels.

<Check question="Which basis states carry amplitude, and what is the state as a tensor product?">
The state is $\ket{1} \otimes \ket{+}$, since $X$ acts on qubit 0 and $H$ on
qubit 1 with nothing linking them. Expanding,

$$
\ket{1}\otimes\ket{+} = \isqrt\left(\ket{10} + \ket{11}\right),
$$

so the amplitudes are $0.707107$ on $\ket{10}$ and $\ket{11}$, and zero on
$\ket{00}$ and $\ket{01}$. Both probabilities are $0.5$. This is a product
state: the test $ad = bc$ on the amplitudes gives $0 \times 0.707107 = 0$ and
$0 \times 0.707107 = 0$, which agree.
</Check>

## Probe 7: reversible computation and complexity

Node: **t0.m6**. Failing this predicts trouble building oracles in T2, where
every one is a reversible circuit with ancillas to clear.

<Check question="How do you get NAND out of a Toffoli gate, why must the ancilla be uncomputed, and which classical class is the honest baseline for a quantum speedup claim?">
Toffoli sends $(a,b,c)$ to $(a, b, c \oplus ab)$. Setting $c = 1$ gives
$1 \oplus ab = \lnot(ab)$, which is NAND, and NAND is universal.

The ancilla must be returned to its initial value because an ancilla still
holding a function of the input is entangled with the computation, and that
entanglement destroys the interference the algorithm relies on. The pattern is
compute, copy the answer out, then run the computation backwards.

The baseline is BPP, not P. Classical computers have always had randomness, so
beating the best deterministic algorithm while tying the best randomised one
demonstrates nothing.
</Check>

## Probe 8: sampling error

Node: **t0.m7**. Failing this predicts reporting a difference between two bars
that the shot count cannot support.

<Check question="How many shots are needed to distinguish a probability of $0.50$ from $0.51$ at two standard errors, and what does an observed count of zero tell you?">
Use $N \ge z^2 p(1-p)/\Delta^2$ with $z = 2$, worst-case $p(1-p) = 0.25$ and
$\Delta = 0.01$:

$$
N \ge \frac{4 \times 0.25}{0.0001} = 10{,}000 .
$$

Comparing two measured probabilities rather than one against a constant doubles
that, because both carry error.

An observed count of zero does not mean the outcome is impossible. The plug-in
formula returns a standard error of zero, which is wrong. Use the rule of three:
zero occurrences in $N$ shots puts the true probability below about $3/N$ with
95 percent confidence, so $0$ in $1000$ shots means below $0.003$.
</Check>

## How the result is written to your graph

Each probe maps to one module node. A correct answer sets that node's mastery
high enough for the scheduler to skip its lessons; an incorrect one leaves the
node unmastered, and every lesson that node gates stays in your path.

| Probes correct | What the scheduler does |
| --- | --- |
| 8 of 8 | All of T0 is skipped; you start at T1.M1 |
| 5 to 7 | Only the modules behind your missed probes are scheduled |
| Below 5 | T0 is scheduled in full, ordered by the prerequisite graph |

<Callout tone="note" title="Skipping is reversible">
Mastery estimates decay, and any T1 lesson that leans on a skipped T0 node can
push it back into your queue when you stumble on it. Nothing here is a one-time
verdict; the graph is a model of you that keeps being updated.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="warning" title="Probe 5 is the one to take seriously">
Every other probe fails loudly: you get an answer you cannot derive and you know
it. Endianness fails quietly. A circuit built under the wrong convention runs,
returns plausible numbers, and is wrong, and the Bell states everyone tests with
are palindromes that look identical either way.
</Callout>
