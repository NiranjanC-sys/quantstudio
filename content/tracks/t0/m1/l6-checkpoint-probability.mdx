Eight questions, one per idea that T0.M2 onwards will assume you already own.
Answer each one before opening it. Six correct unlocks the next module; below
that, the lesson named under each answer is the one to reread, and the tutor
will schedule it first.

Work them on paper. Every number here is small enough to do by hand, and the
point of the exercise is that you can.

## 1. Valid states

<Check question="Which of these are valid probabilistic states of one bit? $a = (0.2, 0.8)$, $b = (0, 1)$, $c = (0.5, 0.5, 0)$ as a state of one bit, $d = (1.2, -0.2)$.">
$a$ and $b$. $a$ is an interior point of the simplex and $b$ is the
deterministic state $\ket{1}$, which is a perfectly good distribution. $c$ has
three entries, so it describes a three-outcome system, not a bit. $d$ sums to 1
but has a negative entry, and negative probability has no operational meaning.

Covered by **T0.M1.L1, Bits and States**.
</Check>

## 2. One step of a chain

<Check question="A bit flips from 0 to 1 with probability $0.1$ and from 1 to 0 with probability $0.4$. Write the operator, then apply it twice to the certain state $\ket{0}$.">
Columns are conditional distributions, so

$$
M = \begin{pmatrix} 0.9 & 0.4 \\ 0.1 & 0.6 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

One step: $M(1,0)^{T} = (0.9, 0.1)^{T}$. Two steps:
$M(0.9, 0.1)^{T} = (0.81 + 0.04,\; 0.09 + 0.06)^{T} = (0.85, 0.15)^{T}$.

Covered by **T0.M1.L2, Coin-Flipping Games**.
</Check>

## 3. The fixed point

<Check question="Where does the chain in question 2 end up after many steps, and how fast does it get there?">
With $a = 0.1$ (the 0 to 1 rate) and $b = 0.4$ (the 1 to 0 rate), the stationary
distribution is $\pi = (b, a)/(a+b) = (0.4, 0.1)/0.5 = (0.8, 0.2)$. Check:
$0.9 \cdot 0.8 + 0.4 \cdot 0.2 = 0.72 + 0.08 = 0.8$. The deviation from $\pi$
shrinks by a factor $1 - a - b = 0.5$ per step, so the gap halves each time.

Covered by **T0.M1.L2, Coin-Flipping Games**.
</Check>

## 4. What makes an operator legal

<Check question="State the two conditions for a column-stochastic matrix, and say in one line why the product of two of them is one.">
Every entry is non-negative, and every column sums to 1, that is
$\mathbf{1}^{T} M = \mathbf{1}^{T}$. For a product,
$\mathbf{1}^{T}(AB) = (\mathbf{1}^{T}A)B = \mathbf{1}^{T}B = \mathbf{1}^{T}$,
and non-negativity survives because the entries of $AB$ are sums of products of
non-negative numbers.

Covered by **T0.M1.L3, Probabilistic Operators and Stochastic Matrices**.
</Check>

## 5. Which operators are reversible

<Check question="Of $I$, the NOT matrix $N$, the fair coin $F$ and the reset $R = \big(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\big)$, which have a stochastic inverse, and what is the general rule?">
Only $I$ and $N$, and each is its own inverse. $F$ and $R$ have rank 1, so they
are not invertible at all. The general rule: a stochastic matrix whose inverse
is also stochastic must be a permutation matrix, because it maps the simplex
bijectively onto itself and therefore permutes the extreme points, which are the
deterministic states.

Covered by **T0.M1.L3, Probabilistic Operators and Stochastic Matrices**.
</Check>

## 6. Marginals and independence

<Check question="For the joint two-bit table $p_{00} = 0.2$, $p_{01} = 0.3$, $p_{10} = 0.3$, $p_{11} = 0.2$, find both marginals, decide whether the bits are independent, and compute the correlation coefficient.">
Row sums: $p_X = (0.5, 0.5)$. Column sums: $p_Y = (0.5, 0.5)$. The outer product
of the marginals is the uniform table $(0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25)$, which is not
the given table, so the bits are dependent. Covariance:
$\expect{X} = \expect{Y} = 0.5$, $\expect{XY} = 0.2$, so
$\operatorname{Cov} = 0.2 - 0.25 = -0.05$ and, since each bit has standard
deviation $0.5$, $\rho = -0.05 / 0.25 = -0.2$. Weakly anticorrelated with
perfectly uniform marginals.

Covered by **T0.M1.L4, Joint Distributions, Marginals, Correlation**.
</Check>

## 7. Entropy by hand

<Check question="Compute $H(0.5, 0.25, 0.25)$ and $H(0.9, 0.1)$ in bits, and state the maximum entropy available on a three-symbol alphabet.">
$H(0.5, 0.25, 0.25) = 0.5 \cdot 1 + 0.25 \cdot 2 + 0.25 \cdot 2 = 1.5$ bits.
$H(0.9, 0.1) = -0.9\log_2 0.9 - 0.1 \log_2 0.1 = 0.9 \cdot 0.152003 +
0.1 \cdot 3.321928 = 0.136803 + 0.332193 = 0.468996$ bits. The maximum on three
symbols is $\log_2 3 = 1.584963$ bits, attained only by the uniform
distribution.

Covered by **T0.M1.L5, Shannon Entropy as a Resource**.
</Check>

## 8. Correlation in bits

<Check question="For the table in question 6, compute the joint entropy and the mutual information $I(X;Y)$.">
The four probabilities are $0.2, 0.3, 0.3, 0.2$, so

$$
H(X,Y) = 2\big(0.2 \log_2 5 + 0.3 \log_2 \tfrac{10}{3}\big) = 1.970951 \text{ bits}.
$$

Both marginals are uniform, so $H(X) = H(Y) = 1$, and

$$
I(X;Y) = 1 + 1 - 1.970951 = 0.029049 \text{ bits}.
$$

Small but non-zero, which is the honest measure of the weak anticorrelation
found in question 6. Compare the perfectly correlated table
$(0.5, 0, 0, 0.5)$, whose mutual information is a full bit.

Covered by **T0.M1.L4 and T0.M1.L5**.
</Check>
