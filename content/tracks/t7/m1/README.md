# T7.M1 Reading the Literature

Quantum results are announced far more often than they are reproduced, and this module builds the reading habit that separates the two. A paper is read for its claim, its assumptions and the regime in which the claim holds, before any derivation is followed, and the resource counts and error model are located early because they decide whether the result is practical. The second lesson turns to benchmarks, distinguishing a measurement of a device from a measurement of a compilation pipeline, and names the three ways an advantage claim is usually weakened. The checkpoint scores abstracts, missing controls and unstated resource assumptions.
