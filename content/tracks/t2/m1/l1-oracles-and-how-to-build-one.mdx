Every algorithm in this track is stated against a black box, so the first
question is what a black box even is when the thing querying it is a quantum
computer. A classical subroutine takes $x$ and returns $f(x)$, destroying $x$ on
the way. A unitary cannot do that: unitaries are invertible, and a map that
forgets its input is not. The standard fix is one line long, and the rest of this
lesson is the price you pay for it.

## The standard oracle

Fix $f: \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$. The **standard oracle** for $f$ acts on $n$ query
qubits and one target qubit as

$$
U_f \ket{x}\ket{y} = \ket{x}\ket{y \oplus f(x)} .
$$

It keeps the input and writes the answer into a target by XOR rather than by
overwrite. Three facts follow immediately.

It is a permutation of the computational basis, so its matrix has exactly one 1
in each row and each column: unitary, for every $f$, with no conditions on $f$
at all. It is its own inverse, since $y \oplus f(x) \oplus f(x) = y$, so
$U_f^2 = I$ and $U_f\dg = U_f$. And it is linear, which is the only interesting
part: apply it to a superposition of inputs and you get

$$
U_f \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n}} \sum_x \ket{x} \right)\ket{0}
= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n}} \sum_x \ket{x}\ket{f(x)} .
$$

<Margin label="One query, not $2^n$">
That state contains every value of $f$, which tempts people to say the computer
evaluated $f$ on all inputs at once. It did not do anything of the kind. Measure
it and you get one random pair $(x, f(x))$, which is exactly what one classical
query gives you. Everything in this track is about arranging *interference* so
that a global property of $f$ concentrates on one measurement outcome.
</Margin>

Take $f(x_0, x_1) = x_0 \wedge x_1$ on two query qubits, target on qubit 2. The
oracle is a single Toffoli, and running it on the uniform superposition of
inputs gives four equally weighted branches whose target bit records the answer.

## Compiling a function into gates

A real oracle is not handed to you; you compile it. The recipe is the one from
T0.M6.L3, and it has three steps.

**Write $f$ as a reversible classical circuit.** Every Boolean function is a
composition of NOT, AND and XOR. NOT is $X$. XOR into a fresh wire is CNOT. AND
into a fresh wire is Toffoli. Nothing else is needed, because Toffoli alone is
universal for reversible computation.

**Give yourself scratch space.** A three-way AND needs a place to put the
two-way AND first. Compute $x_0 \wedge x_1$ into an ancilla $a$ with one Toffoli,
then $a \wedge x_2$ into the target with a second. In general a formula of size
$s$ compiles to $O(s)$ Toffolis and $O(s)$ ancillas.

**Uncompute the scratch space.** Run the ancilla-producing gates again in reverse
order. Toffoli is its own inverse, so the second copy resets $a$ to $\ket{0}$
while leaving the target alone. The whole thing costs a factor of two in gates
and buys back every ancilla.

<Callout tone="note" title="Bennett's trick, stated once">
Compute, copy out, uncompute. If $C$ is a circuit with $C\ket{x}\ket{0}_a =
\ket{x}\ket{g(x)}_a$ where $g(x)$ contains $f(x)$ somewhere, then
$C\dg \cdot \mathrm{CNOT}_{a \to y} \cdot C$ implements $U_f$ exactly, with the
ancillas returned to $\ket{0}$. Time doubles, space is recycled. This is the
standard construction behind every "and therefore $f$ has an oracle" sentence in
the literature.
</Callout>

## Why uncomputation is not optional

Here is the failure you will meet if you skip step three. Take the same
$f = x_0 \wedge x_1$, but this time compute it into an ancilla on qubit 3, XOR
that ancilla into the target on qubit 2, uncompute, and finish with Hadamards on
the query register. The target starts in $\ket{-}$, so kickback turns the oracle
into the sign $(-1)^{f(x)}$.

Now delete the uncompute gate and change nothing else.

The distribution has collapsed onto $\ket{0000}$ and $\ket{0010}$ at $28.125\%$
each, so the query string $00$ now carries $0.625$ and the other three carry
$0.125$. Nothing was measured. The ancilla simply *records* which branch took the
AND, and a record is as good as a measurement: branches that differ in the
ancilla can no longer cancel. Every quantum speedup in this track is a
cancellation, so leaving scratch space behind does not degrade the algorithm, it
deletes it.

<Check question="You are handed a circuit $C$ that computes $f$ into an ancilla and leaves three other ancillas in arbitrary $f$-dependent states. Can you still build $U_f$ from it, and at what cost?">
Yes. Apply $C$, CNOT the answer ancilla onto the target, then apply $C\dg$. The
three garbage ancillas are restored to $\ket{0}$ by $C\dg$ along with everything
else, because $C\dg C = I$ regardless of what $C$ computed. The cost is two calls
to $C$ plus one CNOT, and the ancillas must be clean *before* $C$ runs, which is
why they are called clean ancillas. You do not need to know what the garbage was.
</Check>
