Amplitude amplification is one algorithmic primitive. Quantum walks are the
other, and unlike Grover they are not a single algorithm but a way of turning any
classical Markov-chain algorithm into a quantum one with a square-root saving,
plus, in one celebrated case, an exponential one. The place to start is the walk
on a line, because the difference from a coin-flipping drunkard is visible after
three steps.

## Coin and shift

A classical walker on a $d$-regular graph flips a $d$-sided coin and moves. A
quantum walker cannot: the map "move in a random direction" is not unitary,
because two different vertices can walk to the same place and unitaries do not
merge. The fix is to keep the coin.

The Hilbert space is $\mathcal{H}_C \otimes \mathcal{H}_P$, a $d$-dimensional coin
register tensored with the position register. One step is

$$
U = S \, (C \otimes I) ,
$$

where $C$ is any $d \times d$ unitary on the coin, usually the Hadamard for $d=2$
or the Grover diffusion operator for larger $d$, and $S$ is the **shift**

$$
S \ket{c}\ket{v} = \ket{c}\ket{v + \delta_c} ,
$$

moving the walker in the direction the coin names. $S$ is a permutation of basis
states, hence unitary, and the whole step is unitary because both factors are.

<Margin label="The coin is not overhead">
Keeping the coin is what makes the walk reversible, and reversibility is what
allows amplitudes arriving at the same vertex from different directions to
*interfere*. The classical walk adds probabilities and always spreads; the quantum
walk adds amplitudes, and where they cancel the walker never goes. The coin
register is the memory that makes cancellation possible.
</Margin>

## Three steps on a cycle

Eight positions on a cycle, held in qubits 1 to 3 with qubit 1 the most
significant, and one coin qubit at index 0. The shift decrements the position when
the coin reads 0 and increments it when the coin reads 1, both modulo 8, built from
one three-controlled $X$, one Toffoli and one CNOT per direction.

Sum the five surviving strings over the coin bit and the position distribution is

| position | $-3$ | $-1$ | $+1$ | $+3$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| quantum | $0.125$ | $0.625$ | $0.125$ | $0.125$ |
| classical | $0.125$ | $0.375$ | $0.375$ | $0.125$ |

The quantum walk has piled $0.625$ onto a single position and starved another. The
asymmetry comes from the Hadamard coin, which treats $\ket0$ and $\ket1$
differently: $H\ket1$ carries a minus sign and $H\ket0$ does not, so the
left-moving and right-moving branches interfere with opposite signs. Starting the
coin in $(\ket0 + i\ket1)/\sqrt2$ restores the symmetry, and the distribution
becomes two symmetric peaks near the edges.

## Linear versus square-root spreading

That is the headline. A classical walker after $t$ steps has standard deviation
$\sigma_{\mathrm{cl}} = \sqrt t$, the central limit theorem in its plainest form,
and the distribution is a bell curve centred on the origin. A Hadamard-coin quantum
walker after $t$ steps has

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{qu}} = \Theta(t) ,
$$

with most of the weight in two peaks near $\pm t/\sqrt2$ and almost nothing in the
middle. The walker moves ballistically rather than diffusively.

| $t$ | classical $\sigma$ | quantum $\sigma$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $100$ | $10$ | $\approx 70$ |
| $10^4$ | $100$ | $\approx 7000$ |
| $10^6$ | $1000$ | $\approx 7 \times 10^5$ |

The quadratic gap in spreading rate is where quantum-walk search gets its
quadratic speedup: hitting a marked vertex takes $\sqrt{}$ times fewer steps when
the walker covers distance linearly rather than as a square root.

<Callout tone="gotcha" title="Measure the coin and the speedup evaporates">
Insert a computational-basis measurement of the coin register after every step and
the walk becomes *exactly* the classical random walk: measurement destroys the
relative phase between the two branches, the interference terms vanish, and the
update on the position distribution is the classical stochastic matrix. The
distribution above collapses back to the binomial. This is the cleanest available
demonstration that coherence, not superposition, is the resource: the walker is in
a superposition of positions either way, and only the unmeasured version spreads
linearly.
</Callout>

<Check question="A coined walk on a $d$-regular graph uses a $d$-dimensional coin. What happens if you choose $C = I$?">
The walker never turns. $U = S(I \otimes I) = S$ just applies the same shift every
step, so a walker starting with coin $\ket c$ marches in direction $c$ forever and
the position distribution is a moving delta function. That is maximal ballistic
spreading and zero exploration, which is useless for search. The coin has to mix
the directions, and the standard choices are the Hadamard for $d = 2$ and the
Grover diffusion operator $2\ketbra{s}{s} - I$ on the $d$ directions for larger
$d$: the same operator as T2.M2.L2, now acting on the coin.
</Check>
