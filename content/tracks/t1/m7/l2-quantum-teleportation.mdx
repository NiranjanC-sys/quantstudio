You hold a qubit in an unknown state $\ket{\psi} = \alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1}$.
You cannot measure it without destroying it, you cannot copy it because of
no-cloning, and you cannot describe it in a letter because $\alpha$ and $\beta$
are two continuous real parameters that would take infinitely many bits to write
down. How do you move it to somebody else? The answer costs one shared Bell pair
and exactly two classical bits, and it works with no knowledge of $\alpha$ or
$\beta$ at any point.

## The register layout

Three qubits. Qubit 0 is the message, held by Alice. Qubits 1 and 2 are a Bell
pair $\ket{\Phi^+}$, with qubit 1 at Alice and qubit 2 at Bob. The starting state
is the product

$$
\ket{\psi} \otimes \frac{\ket{00} + \ket{11}}{\sqrt2}.
$$

<Margin label="Convention">
Basis strings read $\ket{q_0 q_1 q_2}$, qubit 0 most significant. Alice measures
qubits 0 and 1 into classical bits $c_0$ and $c_1$; Bob acts on qubit 2.
</Margin>

## The identity, expanded by hand

Expand the product and group the terms by what Alice holds. Writing
$\ket{\psi} = \alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1}$,

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big(
\alpha\ket{0}\ket{00} + \alpha\ket{0}\ket{11}
+ \beta\ket{1}\ket{00} + \beta\ket{1}\ket{11}
\Big).
$$

Now rewrite Alice's two qubits in the Bell basis. Using
$\ket{00} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{\Phi^+} + \ket{\Phi^-})$,
$\ket{11} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{\Phi^+} - \ket{\Phi^-})$,
$\ket{01} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{\Psi^+} + \ket{\Psi^-})$ and
$\ket{10} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{\Psi^+} - \ket{\Psi^-})$ on qubits 0 and 1,
the whole state collapses into four clean terms:

$$
\half\Big(
\ket{\Phi^+}(\alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1})
+ \ket{\Phi^-}(\alpha\ket{0} - \beta\ket{1})
+ \ket{\Psi^+}(\alpha\ket{1} + \beta\ket{0})
+ \ket{\Psi^-}(\alpha\ket{1} - \beta\ket{0})
\Big).
$$

Read the four brackets on the right. They are $\ket{\psi}$, $Z\ket{\psi}$,
$X\ket{\psi}$ and $XZ\ket{\psi}$. The message is already sitting on Bob's qubit
in all four branches, up to one of four known corrections. Nothing has moved yet
and nothing has been measured. This is an algebraic identity about a state that
was always there.

## Turning the Bell basis into bits

Alice cannot measure "in the Bell basis" directly on hardware. She rotates the
Bell basis onto the computational basis with the inverse of the Bell preparation
circuit, CNOT from qubit 0 to qubit 1 followed by Hadamard on qubit 0, then
measures both in the computational basis. Label the outcomes
$c_0$ and $c_1$. The identity above becomes

$$
\half \sum_{c_0, c_1 \in \{0,1\}} \ket{c_0 c_1} \otimes X^{c_1} Z^{c_0}\ket{\psi}.
$$

Every branch has amplitude $\tfrac12$, so each pair of bits appears with
probability $\tfrac14$, independent of $\alpha$ and $\beta$. That independence is
what makes the protocol safe: Alice's outcome carries no information about the
message, which is why she can shout it over an open channel.

Take a concrete message to check the arithmetic. Let
$\ket{\psi} = \cos\tfrac{\pi}{6}\ket{0} + i\sin\tfrac{\pi}{6}\ket{1} = 0.866025\ket{0} + 0.5\,i\ket{1}$, produced by a single $U(\pi/3, \pi/2, 0)$
gate. Here is the state immediately before Alice measures.

Eight non-zero amplitudes, and they are exactly the four branches written out.
The four states whose last qubit is $\ket{0}$ carry amplitude
$\alpha/2 = 0.4330$, and the four whose last qubit is $\ket{1}$ carry
$\pm\beta/2 = \pm 0.25\,i$. Group them by the first two labels:

| $c_0 c_1$ | Bob's qubit | correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $00$ | $\alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1}$ | none |
| $01$ | $\beta\ket{0} + \alpha\ket{1}$ | $X$ |
| $10$ | $\alpha\ket{0} - \beta\ket{1}$ | $Z$ |
| $11$ | $-\beta\ket{0} + \alpha\ket{1}$ | $X$ then $Z$ |

So Bob applies $X$ if $c_1 = 1$, then $Z$ if $c_0 = 1$. Two bits, four cases, no
knowledge of the message required.

Two rows survive, and their probabilities are $0.75$ and $0.25$: qubit 2 now has
exactly the measurement statistics of the message. Qubits 0 and 1 are frozen at
whichever pair of bits this run produced.

</Simulate>

This circuit contains classical conditions, so the simulator runs a single
trajectory rather than an exact statevector, and it reports magnitudes only.
That is honest: once a measurement has happened there is no single global
amplitude vector to report. Run it and read qubit 2: whichever branch fired,
its marginal is $0.75$ on $\ket{0}$ and $0.25$ on $\ket{1}$, which is
$|\alpha|^2$ and $|\beta|^2$ for the message we prepared.

<Callout tone="gotcha" title="Nothing travels faster than light">
Before Bob receives $c_0$ and $c_1$, his qubit is in the maximally mixed state,
averaged over the four equally likely branches. He can measure it all he likes and
learn nothing. The protocol completes only when two classical bits arrive over an
ordinary channel, at ordinary speed.
</Callout>

## Two costs that are not optional

**The original is destroyed.** Alice measured qubit 0. Whatever it was, it is now
a classical bit. If teleportation left the original intact it would be a cloning
machine, and T1.M5.L4 proved no such machine exists. The destruction is not a
side effect of a clumsy implementation; it is what keeps the protocol consistent.

**Two classical bits are required.** One bit would leave Bob unable to
distinguish two of the four branches, and his best guess would be wrong half the
time on those. You cannot compress below two because the four corrections
$I$, $X$, $Z$, $XZ$ are genuinely different operations on an unknown state.

The trade is now visible next to superdense coding. There, one qubit plus one
Bell pair bought two classical bits. Here, two classical bits plus one Bell pair
buy one qubit. Entanglement is the currency both directions convert through.

<Check question="Alice runs the protocol but the classical channel fails and Bob never learns $c_0 c_1$. What state does Bob hold?">
The maximally mixed state $\tfrac12 I$, for any message whatsoever. Averaging
$X^{c_1}Z^{c_0}\ket{\psi}$ over the four equally likely branches gives the same
mixture regardless of $\alpha$ and $\beta$, which is exactly the statement that
the two classical bits carry all of the information Bob is missing. His qubit is
useless until they arrive, and no amount of local processing fixes that.
</Check>
