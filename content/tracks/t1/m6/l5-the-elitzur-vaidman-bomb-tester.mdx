A crate holds a bomb whose trigger is a single photon: absorb one and it
detonates. Some crates are duds, with a broken trigger that lets photons through.
You must sort the live bombs from the duds without setting any off. Classically
this is impossible, since the only way to learn whether the trigger absorbs
photons is to send one. Quantum mechanically it can be done, and with enough
patience it can be done almost every time.

## The one-shot version

Model the photon's path as a qubit: $\ket{0}$ is the reference arm and $\ket{1}$
is the arm containing the crate. A beamsplitter is a Hadamard. The interferometer
is $H$, then the crate, then $H$ again, then look at which output port the photon
leaves by.

With a **dud**, nothing touches the photon and the two Hadamards cancel:
$H H \ket{0} = \ket{0}$. The photon always leaves by port 0.

With a **live bomb** the crate is a detector: it records whether the photon took
arm 1. Model the record as a second qubit and the recording as a CNOT from the
path onto it, which is what T1.M5.L3 called entangling with the environment.
Then the circuit is $H$ on the path, CNOT onto the bomb, $H$ on the path.

Read the four outcomes by what they mean.

| outcome | probability | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\ket{01}$, $\ket{11}$ | $0.25 + 0.25 = 0.5$ | the bomb absorbed the photon and detonated |
| $\ket{00}$ | $0.25$ | port 0 fired: consistent with a dud, inconclusive |
| $\ket{10}$ | $0.25$ | port 1 fired with no absorption |

The last row is the interesting one. Port 1 can never fire for a dud, because
interference sends every dud photon to port 0. So port 1 firing proves the bomb
is live, and it fired on a run in which the bomb absorbed nothing. You have
learned that the trigger works without triggering it, with probability $0.25$
per attempt, against a $0.5$ chance of losing the bomb.

<Margin label="Why interference is doing the work">
The dud case has one path amplitude for reaching port 1 from each arm, and they
cancel. The live bomb destroys that cancellation by recording which arm was
taken, so the two contributions no longer interfere and port 1 becomes
reachable. The bomb changes the outcome distribution purely by being *able* to
record, in the branch where it records nothing.
</Margin>

## Zeno amplification

A quarter success against a half loss is a bad trade. Replace the single
beamsplitter with a slow rotation applied $N$ times, with the crate consulted
after each step.

Each cycle applies $R_y(\pi/N)$, which moves the photon's amplitude by an angle
$\pi/(2N)$ from $\ket{0}$ towards $\ket{1}$. The bomb then records. In the branch
where nothing was absorbed, the photon is projected back onto $\ket{0}$, and the
next cycle starts from where the last one began.

The probability of absorption in one cycle is $\sin^2\!\left(\pi/(2N)\right)$, so
the probability of surviving all $N$ cycles is

$$
\Pr[\text{survive}] = \cos^{2N}\!\left( \frac{\pi}{2N} \right) .
$$

For small $\pi/(2N)$ the per-cycle loss is about $\pi^2/(4N^2)$, and $N$ of them
total about $\pi^2/(4N)$, which goes to 0 as $N$ grows. Meanwhile a dud never
records anything, so the $N$ rotations compose into $R_y(\pi)$ and leave the
photon in $\ket{1}$ with certainty. Survive the run and the final measurement
tells you which crate you had: $\ket{0}$ means live, $\ket{1}$ means dud.

| $N$ | survival probability |
| --- | --- |
| $3$ | $0.421875$ |
| $4$ | $0.530790$ |
| $10$ | $0.780546$ |
| $50$ | $0.951842$ |
| $100$ | $0.975627$ |

Take $N = 3$ and check it exactly. Each cycle survives with
$\cos^2(\pi/6) = 0.75$, so three survive with $0.75^3 = 27/64 = 0.421875$.

The amplitude on $\ket{0000}$ is $\cos^3(\pi/6) = 0.649519$, whose square is
$0.421875$. Note that no listed outcome has the photon at $\ket{1}$ with all
records at 0: with a live bomb, surviving means ending at $\ket{0}$. The dud
runs the same three rotations with no CNOTs, giving $R_y(\pi)\ket{0} = \ket{1}$
with probability 1.

## What "interaction-free" means

Three honest qualifications, because the phrase oversells the result.

**No energy was absorbed on the successful branch.** That is the precise claim,
and it is true: in the branch where port 1 fires or where all records stay 0, the
bomb absorbed no photon.

**The bomb's *presence* certainly had an effect.** The outcome distribution
changed, which is how you learned anything at all. What the bomb did was destroy
the interference by being capable of recording. Some authors prefer
"absorption-free" or "quantum interrogation" for exactly this reason.

**The successful branch is selected after the fact.** You do not choose to be in
it. With $N = 3$ you lose the bomb $0.578125$ of the time, and the protocol only
looks impressive once $N$ is large enough that the loss is rare.

<Callout tone="gotcha" title="It is the same mechanism as decoherence">
Nothing here is specific to bombs. Replace the bomb with any environment that
could record which path was taken and the interference disappears, whether or
not anyone reads the record. That is exactly the decoherence of T1.M11, seen
from the other side: here the loss of interference is the signal rather than the
noise.
</Callout>

<Check question="With $N = 4$ cycles, what is the per-cycle absorption probability and the overall survival probability?">
Each cycle is $R_y(\pi/4)$, so the amplitude on $\ket{1}$ after one rotation is
$\sin(\pi/8) = 0.382683$ and the absorption probability is
$\sin^2(\pi/8) = 0.146447$. Survival per cycle is
$\cos^2(\pi/8) = 0.853553$, and over four cycles
$0.853553^4 = 0.530790$. Going from $N = 3$ to $N = 4$ raises the success rate
from $0.421875$ to $0.530790$, and the improvement continues, slowly, like
$1 - \pi^2/(4N)$.
</Check>
