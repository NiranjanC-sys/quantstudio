You have spent eleven modules taking other people's word for what a Bell state is.
This project is the one where you produce the numbers yourself: prepare all four
Bell states, measure them in three bases, turn the counts into a fidelity with an
honest error bar, run CHSH, and write down what your data does and does not
establish. It is the experiment every hardware team runs on a new device, and it
is deliberately the same shape as the benchmarking work in T3.M7.

## Deliverables

1. A module that builds each of the four Bell states as a circuit in the platform
   IR and asserts its statevector against the reference below.
2. A measurement routine that estimates $\expect{ZZ}$, $\expect{XX}$ and
   $\expect{YY}$ from counts at a stated shot budget.
3. A fidelity estimate for $\ket{\Phi^+}$ with a $1\sigma$ error bar derived from
   shot noise.
4. A CHSH value with an error bar, and a one-sentence statement of whether it
   exceeds 2 by more than your uncertainty.
5. A short report, at most two pages, including one plot with error bars and a
   paragraph on what your result does not prove.

## Part 1: the four states

Qubit 0 is the most significant bit. Each circuit is the Hadamard and CNOT pair
plus at most two extra single-qubit gates.

| state | circuit after H on q0 and CNOT q0 to q1 | amplitudes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\ket{\Phi^+}$ | nothing | $\ket{00}: +0.7071$, $\ket{11}: +0.7071$ |
| $\ket{\Phi^-}$ | $Z$ on q0 | $\ket{00}: +0.7071$, $\ket{11}: -0.7071$ |
| $\ket{\Psi^+}$ | $X$ on q1 | $\ket{01}: +0.7071$, $\ket{10}: +0.7071$ |
| $\ket{\Psi^-}$ | $Z$ on q0 and $X$ on q1 | $\ket{01}: +0.7071$, $\ket{10}: -0.7071$ |

Assert against the amplitude vector up to global phase, and assert the resource
bound too: two gates, depth 2, exactly one two-qubit gate. A submission that
prepares the right state with five gates should score lower than one that uses
two, for the reasons T1.M11.L5 made quantitative.

## Part 2: three bases

A computational-basis measurement gives you $\expect{ZZ}$ and nothing else, which
is not enough to identify a Bell state: $\ket{\Phi^+}$ and the classically
correlated mixture of $\ket{00}$ and $\ket{11}$ have identical $Z$-basis counts.
Rotate before measuring.

| observable | gates before measurement | expected value on $\ket{\Phi^+}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\expect{ZZ}$ | none | $+1$ |
| $\expect{XX}$ | $H$ on both qubits | $+1$ |
| $\expect{YY}$ | $S\dg$ then $H$ on both qubits | $-1$ |

Each correlator is computed from the counts as

$$
\expect{AB} = \frac{n_{00} - n_{01} - n_{10} + n_{11}}{N},
$$

the probability of agreement minus the probability of disagreement.

<Margin label="Sanity check">
On the noiseless simulator the $XX$ circuit returns $\ket{00}$ and $\ket{11}$ at
$0.5$ each, and the $YY$ circuit returns $\ket{01}$ and $\ket{10}$ at $0.5$ each.
If your $YY$ correlator comes out $+1$ you have the sign of the $S\dg$ wrong.
</Margin>

## Part 3: fidelity with an error bar

For $\ket{\Phi^+}$ the density matrix decomposes as
$\ketbra{\Phi^+}{\Phi^+} = \tfrac14(II + XX - YY + ZZ)$, so the fidelity of a
measured state $\rho$ with the target is

$$
F = \frac{1 + \expect{XX} - \expect{YY} + \expect{ZZ}}{4}.
$$

Three correlators, three circuits, one number. On the ideal state that is
$(1 + 1 + 1 + 1)/4 = 1$.

The error bar comes from shot noise, which you met in T1.M2.L5. A correlator
estimated from $N$ shots has standard error
$\sqrt{(1 - \expect{AB}^2)/N}$, and the three are measured on independent circuits,
so

$$
\sigma_F = \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\sigma_{XX}^2 + \sigma_{YY}^2 + \sigma_{ZZ}^2}.
$$

Choose $N$ deliberately and say why. For a correlator near $0.7071$, a shot budget
of $4096$ gives a standard error of $0.011049$ per correlator; $1024$ shots gives
$0.022097$ and $8192$ gives $0.007812$. Quadrupling the shots halves the error
bar, which is the only lever shot noise responds to.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Do not report a fidelity without a bar">
A fidelity quoted to four decimals from 1000 shots is four decimals of which two
are noise. The grading rubric weights the error bar as heavily as the estimate,
and a correct estimate with no uncertainty scores below a slightly worse estimate
with a defensible one.
</Callout>

## Part 4: CHSH

Measure each qubit along a direction in the $x$-$z$ plane at angle $\theta$,
implemented as $R_y(-\theta)$ followed by a computational measurement. On
$\ket{\Phi^+}$ the correlator is $E(\theta_A, \theta_B) = \cos(\theta_A - \theta_B)$.

Use the standard settings $\theta_a = 0$, $\theta_{a'} = \pi/2$,
$\theta_b = \pi/4$, $\theta_{b'} = -\pi/4$, and form

$$
S = E(a,b) + E(a,b') + E(a',b) - E(a',b').
$$

Each of the four correlators is $\pm 0.707107$, and the combination gives
$S = 2.828427$, which is $2\sqrt2$. The classical bound is $2$, so the ideal
violation is $0.828427$.

The error bar on $S$ follows from the four independent correlators. With
$E \approx 0.7071$ each has standard error $\sqrt{0.5/N}$, so

$$
\sigma_S = 2\sqrt{\frac{0.5}{N}},
$$

which is $0.044194$ at $N = 1024$, $0.022097$ at $N = 4096$ and $0.015625$ at
$N = 8192$. Report $S$ as an estimate plus or minus $\sigma_S$ and state how many
standard errors it sits above 2.

## Part 5: the honest paragraph

The report must include a paragraph on what your numbers do not show. Three
things belong in it, and the rubric checks for them.

**Simulation is not evidence about hardware.** If you ran on the browser
simulator you measured your own arithmetic. Say so.

**A CHSH violation on a single device with a shared control system is not a
loophole-free Bell test.** The detection loophole and the locality loophole both
require experimental arrangements a gate-model quantum computer does not provide.
Your measurement demonstrates that the correlations exceed the classical bound in
your data; it does not close the loopholes.

**Fidelity is one number and it hides structure.** A fidelity of $0.92$ is
consistent with many different error mechanisms. T1.M11.L4 gives the local
depolarising reading, $p = 0.054837$ for that value, but coherent over-rotation
would produce the same fidelity with a completely different Bloch-sphere picture,
and only a depth sweep or full tomography separates them.

## Rubric

| criterion | weight |
| --- | --- |
| All four Bell states correct, verified up to global phase | 20 |
| Resource bounds met: 2 gates, depth 2, one two-qubit gate | 10 |
| Three correlators measured with correct basis-change circuits | 20 |
| Fidelity estimate with a correctly derived shot-noise error bar | 20 |
| CHSH value, error bar, and a statement of significance | 20 |
| The honest paragraph, with all three points addressed | 10 |

<Check question="Your CHSH result comes out at $S = 2.79 \pm 0.02$. A reviewer says this disproves local realism. What do you say?">
That the number is $39.5$ standard errors above the classical bound of 2 and is
strong evidence that your data cannot be reproduced by the local hidden-variable
model CHSH assumes, and that this is not the same claim. A gate-model device
measures both qubits with one control system, in one cryostat, without spacelike
separation and without closing the detection loophole, so a local hidden-variable
model that exploits either loophole reproduces your data comfortably. The correct
sentence is that the observed correlations violate the CHSH bound by
$0.79 \pm 0.02$ under the assumption that the device behaves as modelled, which is a
statement about the device rather than about nature.
</Check>
