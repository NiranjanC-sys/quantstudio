The previous lesson proved that nothing Alice does changes Bob's statistics.
That proof needed a sum over Alice's outcomes at every step, which is clumsy.
There is an object that packages the sum once and for all: the **reduced state**
of Bob's qubit. It is not a vector, and learning to accept that is the price of
being able to talk about half of an entangled pair at all.

## Half of a Bell pair is not a vector

Ask the obvious question: what state is qubit 1 of $\ket{\Phi^+}$ in? Every
candidate fails. It is not $\ket{0}$, since it reads 1 half the time. It is not
$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{0} + \ket{1})$, since measuring it in the $X$ basis is a
coin flip too, while $\ket{+}$ would give $+$ with certainty. It is not any
vector at all, because every one-qubit vector is certain about some measurement
and this qubit is certain about none.

The Bloch picture makes the failure visible. Every state vector maps to an arrow
of length 1 on the surface of the sphere. This qubit's arrow has length 0.

## Density matrices in one page

Replace the vector $\ket{\psi}$ by the matrix

$$
\rho = \ketbra{\psi}{\psi} ,
$$

which contains the same information: it is unchanged by a global phase, and the
Born rule becomes $\Pr[m] = \bra{m}\rho\ket{m}$. For $\ket{0}$ it is
$\mathrm{diag}(1, 0)$, and for $\ket{+}$ it is
$\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1\end{pmatrix}$.

The gain is that matrices can be averaged and vectors cannot. A source emitting
$\ket{0}$ or $\ket{1}$ with equal probability is described by
$\tfrac12\ketbra{0}{0} + \tfrac12\ketbra{1}{1} = \tfrac{I}{2}$, and no vector
does that job. Such a $\rho$ is called **mixed**; a $\rho$ of the form
$\ketbra{\psi}{\psi}$ is **pure**.

<Margin label="Bloch, extended">
Every one-qubit density matrix is
$\rho = \tfrac12\left( I + r_x X + r_y Y + r_z Z \right)$ with
$\lVert \vec{r} \rVert \le 1$. Length 1 is pure and on the surface; length 0 is
$I/2$, the maximally mixed state at the centre. The figures above report that
length, which is why T1.M2.L4 could estimate a state from three counts.
</Margin>

## The partial trace

To get the state of qubit $B$ from a joint $\rho$, sum out $A$:

$$
\left( \rho_B \right)_{jk} = \sum_{a \in \{0, 1\}} \bra{a j} \rho \ket{a k} .
$$

That is the partial trace, written $\rho_B = \tr_A(\rho)$. It is exactly the sum
over Alice's unrecorded outcomes from the previous lesson, done once instead of
per measurement.

Run it on the Bell state. With
$\rho = \tfrac12\left( \ketbra{00}{00} + \ketbra{00}{11} + \ketbra{11}{00} + \ketbra{11}{11} \right)$,

$$
(\rho_B)_{00} = \bra{00}\rho\ket{00} + \bra{10}\rho\ket{10} = \tfrac12 + 0 = \tfrac12 ,
$$

$$
(\rho_B)_{01} = \bra{00}\rho\ket{01} + \bra{10}\rho\ket{11} = 0 + 0 = 0 ,
$$

and symmetrically $(\rho_B)_{11} = \tfrac12$, $(\rho_B)_{10} = 0$. So

$$
\rho_B = \frac{I}{2} .
$$

Maximally mixed. Its Bloch vector is $(0,0,0)$, which is the length-0 arrow in
the figure, and it says every measurement on Bob's qubit in every basis is a
fair coin. The off-diagonal terms $\ketbra{00}{11}$ contributed nothing because
they connect states that differ in Bob's index as well as Alice's, and the
partial trace only pairs terms that agree on Alice's index.

Now run it on the product state $\ket{+}\otimes\ket{0}$, where
$\rho = \ketbra{+}{+} \otimes \ketbra{0}{0}$. The sum factors and gives
$\rho_A = \ketbra{+}{+}$ and $\rho_B = \ketbra{0}{0}$: the original factors come
back exactly, with Bloch vectors $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,0,1)$. That is the general
rule, $\tr_B(\rho_A \otimes \rho_B) = \rho_A$, and it confirms that the partial
trace is the right generalisation of "just look at one part".

## Mixedness measures entanglement

For a pure joint state, the reduced state is pure exactly when the joint state
is a product. So how mixed the halves are is a measure of how entangled the
whole is, and it runs from arrow length 1 for a product state down to arrow
length 0 for a Bell pair. T1.M5.L5 turns that observation into a number.

<Callout tone="gotcha" title="Mixed is not the same as merely unknown">
The reduced state of a Bell half is $I/2$, and so is the state of a qubit that
was secretly prepared as $\ket{0}$ or $\ket{1}$ by a coin flip. The two
situations are locally identical and globally different: in the first case the
correlation with the partner qubit is stronger than any classical variable
allows, and in the second there is a fact about which state was prepared. A
density matrix records everything local and deliberately forgets the rest.
</Callout>

## Why the reduced state is the right object

Three properties, and together they justify the definition.

It reproduces all local statistics: $\Pr[b] = \bra{b}\rho_B\ket{b}$ and, more
generally, $\expect{I \otimes A} = \tr(\rho_B A)$ for any observable $A$ on
Bob's side.

It is invariant under anything Alice does. Applying $U \otimes I$ to the joint
state leaves $\rho_B$ unchanged, which is the no-signalling theorem of T1.M5.L2
compressed into one line, since the $U$ and $U^{\dagger}$ meet under the trace.

It is the *only* object with those properties. Any two joint states with the
same $\rho_B$ are indistinguishable to Bob, and any two with different $\rho_B$
are distinguishable given enough copies. So $\rho_B$ is precisely Bob's
information, no more and no less.

<Check question="Compute the reduced state of qubit 1 for $\ket{\chi} = 0.6\ket{00} + 0.8\ket{11}$, and give its Bloch vector.">
The joint density matrix has entries $0.36$ on $\ketbra{00}{00}$, $0.64$ on
$\ketbra{11}{11}$, and $0.48$ on each of $\ketbra{00}{11}$ and
$\ketbra{11}{00}$. Tracing out qubit 0 gives
$(\rho_B)_{00} = 0.36$, $(\rho_B)_{11} = 0.64$ and off-diagonals 0, so
$\rho_B = \mathrm{diag}(0.36, 0.64)$. Its Bloch vector is
$(0,\ 0,\ 0.36 - 0.64) = (0, 0, -0.28)$, of length $0.28$: mixed but not
maximally so, matching the concurrence of $0.96$ from T1.M4.L3, which said this
state is entangled but not maximally entangled.
</Check>
