## The intended solution

Recognise the bond exponential as a fractional SWAP, then build the fractional
SWAP in three gates.

$XX + YY + ZZ$ has eigenvalue $+1$ on the three triplet states and $-3$ on the
singlet. $\mathrm{SWAP}$ has eigenvalue $+1$ on the triplet and $-1$ on the
singlet. Two operators with the same eigenvectors and a linear relation between
the eigenvalues, so

$$
e^{-i\theta(XX+YY+ZZ)/2} = e^{-i\theta/2}\;\mathrm{SWAP}^{\,2\theta/\pi},
$$

with $\alpha = 2\theta/\pi = 1/3$ at $\theta = \pi/6$.

Now use the fact that $\mathrm{SWAP}$ is a conjugation:
$\mathrm{SWAP} = \mathrm{CNOT}(i \to j)\;\mathrm{CNOT}(j \to i)\;\mathrm{CNOT}(i \to j)$,
and conjugation commutes with taking powers, so only the middle factor is raised:

$$
\mathrm{SWAP}^{\alpha} = \mathrm{CNOT}(i \to j)\;\mathrm{CNOT}(j \to i)^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{CNOT}(i \to j).
$$

$\mathrm{CNOT}^{\alpha}$ is a controlled $R_x(\pi\alpha)$ together with a phase
$P(\pi\alpha/2)$ on the control wire, and the phase is a single-qubit gate. So
each bond is CNOT, controlled $R_x(\pi/3)$, phase, CNOT: three two-qubit gates.
Two bonds, six two-qubit gates, eight ops, depth 8.

Applied to $\ket{100}$ the step gives $0.75$ on $\ket{100}$, $0.1875$ on
$\ket{010}$ and $0.0625$ on $\ket{001}$: the magnon has started to hop, and no
weight leaves the single-excitation sector because the Heisenberg interaction
commutes with total $Z$.

## Why the naive answer costs double

The obvious construction applies the three Pauli terms of each bond separately:
$R_{ZZ}(\theta)$, $R_{XX}(\theta)$, $R_{YY}(\theta)$, each of which compiles to
CNOT, single-qubit rotation, CNOT. Six two-qubit gates per bond, twelve for the
step, against a budget of six.

Peephole simplification helps a little and not enough. Adjacent terms on the same
bond share their basis-change layers, and the inner CNOT pair of consecutive
$R_{PP}$ blocks cancels, which takes twelve down to about eight. Getting to six
requires the structural observation that the three terms together form a root of
SWAP, which no local rewrite will find for you.

Multiply the saving by the number of Trotter steps a real simulation runs, which
is thousands, and by the number of bonds in a real lattice, and the factor of two
is the difference between a circuit that fits on hardware and one that does not.

## Why the global phase is allowed here

The identity carries $e^{-i\theta/2}$ per bond, so the circuit built from
fractional SWAPs differs from the mathematical Trotter step by $e^{i\theta}$
overall, $e^{i\pi/6}$ at this step size. That is unobservable, and it stays
unobservable when the step is repeated, because repeating multiplies the same
scalar. If you ever make the step *controlled*, for instance inside phase
estimation, the scalar becomes a relative phase and has to be tracked, which is
why the identity is worth writing down with its prefactor rather than waving at.

## Common wrong answers

Applying the bonds in the other order, $(1,2)$ before $(0,1)$, gives a different
unitary. First-order Trotter is order dependent by construction: that is the
entire source of its error, and the problem fixes the order so that the answer is
well defined.

Using $\alpha = \theta$ rather than $2\theta/\pi$ is the usual arithmetic slip.
It produces a legitimate fractional SWAP at the wrong power: applied to
$\ket{100}$ it gives $0.462965$, $0.248628$ and $0.288407$ where the correct step
gives $0.75$, $0.1875$ and $0.0625$. The single-excitation sector is still
respected, so the structure looks right and every number is wrong in the first
decimal place.
