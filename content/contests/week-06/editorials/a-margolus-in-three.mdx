## The intended solution

Four quarter turns and three CNOTs:

```text
ry( pi/4)  2
CNOT 0 -> 2
ry( pi/4)  2
CNOT 1 -> 2
ry(-pi/4)  2
CNOT 0 -> 2
ry(-pi/4)  2
```

Seven ops, depth 7, three CNOTs. The operator agrees with the Toffoli everywhere
except the diagonal entry at $\ket{011}$, which is $-1$ instead of $+1$.

The mechanism is conjugation. $X R_y(\phi) X = R_y(-\phi)$, so a CNOT sitting
before a rotation flips that rotation's sign in exactly the branches where its
control reads 1. Materialise the operator and read off the $2 \times 2$ block it
applies to qubit 2 in each of the four control branches:

| $x_0 x_1$ | block on qubit 2 |
| --- | --- |
| $00$ | $I$ |
| $01$ | $Z$ |
| $10$ | $I$ |
| $11$ | $X$ |

Only the $11$ branch accumulates a full $\pi$ of rotation, which is the $X$ the
Toffoli is supposed to apply. Two of the other three branches cancel completely.
The $01$ branch cancels as a rotation and leaves a $Z$ behind, and that $Z$ is
the whole difference between this gate and the Toffoli: it is the $-1$ at entry
$(011, 011)$ and nothing else.

## Why the naive answer is too expensive

The true Toffoli is six CNOTs and seven $T$ gates, and six CNOTs is the proven
minimum for an exact ancilla-free implementation. Three is not a smarter version
of that: it is a different gate. The insight is knowing when a different gate is
good enough.

The answer is: whenever the Toffoli appears in a compute and uncompute pair
around a scratch register. If the same relative phase is introduced by the
compute and removed by the uncompute, the pair is exactly equivalent to two true
Toffolis, and you have halved the two-qubit gate count of that block for free.
Multi-controlled gate constructions, comparators, and most oracle scaffolding are
built out of exactly such pairs.

## Common wrong answers

Getting the CNOT order wrong is the whole failure surface here. Keeping the sign
pattern $+, +, -, -$ but controlling the three CNOTs from qubits 0, 0 and 1
instead of 0, 1 and 0 leaves the $11$ branch correct and breaks the other two:
the $01$ and $10$ branches come out as Hadamard-like blocks with every entry at
$0.707107$, so the gate is not a permutation matrix at all. Any entry of modulus
$0.707107$ anywhere in the operator means the branch bookkeeping is wrong.

Using $\pi/2$ rotations instead of $\pi/4$ makes each branch overshoot by a
factor of two, and the result is a clean permutation matrix that is not the gate
you wanted: it flips qubit 2 whenever qubit 1 reads 1, regardless of qubit 0,
which is a plain CNOT from wire 1. It is a good reminder that a permutation
matrix is not evidence of correctness.

Submitting the true Toffoli, whether by writing `ccx` or by building the six-CNOT
circuit, is rejected twice: once by the gate-set check or the CNOT bound, and
once by the check that entry $(011, 011)$ is $-1$. This problem wants the cheap
gate, not the correct one.
