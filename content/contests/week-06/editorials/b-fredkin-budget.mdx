## The intended solution

Two identities, composed.

The first turns an exchange into a flip:

$$
\mathrm{CSWAP}(0;\,1,2) = \mathrm{CNOT}(2 \to 1)\;\mathrm{CCX}(0,1 \to 2)\;\mathrm{CNOT}(2 \to 1).
$$

Trace it on basis states. The first CNOT leaves qubit 1 holding $b \oplus c$. The
Toffoli flips qubit 2 by $a \wedge (b \oplus c)$, so qubit 2 becomes
$c \oplus a(b \oplus c)$, which is $c$ when $a = 0$ and $b$ when $a = 1$. The
second CNOT then makes qubit 1 hold $(b \oplus c) \oplus$ its new partner, which
is $b$ when $a = 0$ and $c$ when $a = 1$. Exchange when the control is set, and
nothing otherwise.

The second identity turns the Toffoli into a CCZ:
$\mathrm{CCX}(0,1 \to 2) = H_2\,\mathrm{CCZ}\,H_2$, and the CCZ is the
seven-$T$, six-CNOT circuit from Week 1 Problem D.

Total: two outer CNOTs, two Hadamards, and the thirteen-op CCZ, giving 17 ops,
depth 13, eight two-qubit gates and a T-count of seven. The operator is exactly
CSWAP, no global phase.

## Why the naive answers are too expensive

Three of them show up.

Writing `cswap` directly is rejected by the gate set, for the same reason `ccz`
was rejected in Week 1: a fault-tolerant machine has no such instruction.

Building the controlled SWAP as three controlled-CNOTs, one per CNOT of the
SWAP, gives three Toffolis where one suffices. That is a T-count of 21 and
eighteen CNOTs, roughly three times the budget on both axes, and it is by far the
most common overspend because it follows so naturally from "a SWAP is three
CNOTs, so a controlled SWAP is three controlled CNOTs".

Running a generic three-qubit synthesis routine on the $8 \times 8$ matrix
produces a correct circuit with a T-count in the dozens, because generic
synthesis has no way to notice the permutation structure.

## Why the outer CNOTs are free

Worth noticing: the two outer CNOTs cost two two-qubit gates and zero $T$ gates.
Under an error-correcting code the $T$ gates dominate the cost by orders of
magnitude, so a rewrite that trades three Toffolis for one Toffoli plus two
CNOTs cuts the real cost by a factor of three, and the two extra CNOTs do not
register.

## Common wrong answers

Conjugating with $\mathrm{CNOT}(1 \to 2)$ instead of $\mathrm{CNOT}(2 \to 1)$
puts the parity on the wrong wire, and the two outer CNOTs then cancel each other
around the Toffoli instead of folding it. What comes back is the bare Toffoli:
$110$ and $111$ exchanged, $101$ and $110$ untouched. The gate is a legitimate
Toffoli and it is not a Fredkin.

Putting the Hadamards on qubit 1 rather than on the Toffoli's target, qubit 2,
turns the CCZ into a controlled flip aimed at the wrong wire. The result is still
a permutation matrix, which makes it look plausible, and it exchanges $101$ with
$111$ rather than $101$ with $110$.
