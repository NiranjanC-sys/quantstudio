Hamiltonian simulation is the application quantum computers were invented for,
and its inner loop is a Trotter step: chop the time evolution into pieces small
enough that the non-commuting terms can be applied one after another. The cost of
the whole simulation is the cost of that step multiplied by a very large number,
so a factor of two in the step is a factor of two in the whole run. This problem
is one step of the isotropic Heisenberg chain, with the budget set at the good
construction rather than the obvious one.

## Task

Let

$$
H = \sum_{(i,j)} \left( X_i X_j + Y_i Y_j + Z_i Z_j \right)
$$

over the two bonds $(0,1)$ and $(1,2)$ of a three-site open chain. Return a
three-qubit circuit implementing one first-order Trotter step at
$\theta = \pi/6$,

$$
U = e^{-i\theta H_{12}/2}\; e^{-i\theta H_{01}/2},
$$

with the bond $(0,1)$ applied first, up to a global phase, using at most six
two-qubit gates.

## Input and output contract

Implement one function, taking no arguments:

```python
def solve() -> dict:
    """Return a RedQuant circuit IR, version 1."""
```

Ops are applied in list order. A controlled $R_x$ is the `crx` gate, whose first
target is the control:

```python
{"id": "b0b", "gate": "crx", "targets": [1, 0], "params": [1.0471975511965976]}
```

applies $R_x(\pi/3)$ to qubit 0 when qubit 1 is $\ket 1$. A `p` gate is
$\mathrm{diag}(1, e^{i\lambda})$ and counts as a single-qubit gate.

Qubit 0 is the most significant bit, so $X_0 X_1$ acts on the top two bits of the
basis index.

## Worked example

The bond Hamiltonian $XX + YY + ZZ$ has eigenvalue $+1$ on the triplet and $-3$
on the singlet, which makes its exponential a fractional SWAP:

$$
e^{-i\theta(XX+YY+ZZ)/2} = e^{-i\theta/2}\,\mathrm{SWAP}^{\,2\theta/\pi}.
$$

At $\theta = \pi/6$ the exponent is $\alpha = 1/3$. Since
$\mathrm{SWAP} = \mathrm{CNOT}(i \to j)\,\mathrm{CNOT}(j \to i)\,\mathrm{CNOT}(i \to j)$
is a conjugation of one CNOT by another, raising it to a power raises only the
middle factor:
$\mathrm{SWAP}^{\alpha} = \mathrm{CNOT}(i \to j)\,\mathrm{CNOT}(j \to i)^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{CNOT}(i \to j)$,
and the fractional CNOT is one controlled $R_x(\pi\alpha)$ plus a phase
$P(\pi\alpha/2)$ on the control wire. Three two-qubit gates per bond, six for the
step.

Applying the reference circuit to $\ket{100}$, one magnon at the left end of the
chain, gives:

| basis state | amplitude | probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\ket{100}$ | $0.75 + 0.433013i$ | $0.75$ |
| $\ket{010}$ | $0.375 - 0.216506i$ | $0.1875$ |
| $\ket{001}$ | $-0.125 - 0.216506i$ | $0.0625$ |

The excitation has begun to hop right, weight stays entirely inside the
single-magnon sector because the Heisenberg interaction conserves total $Z$, and
the three probabilities sum to 1. The circuit has 8 ops, depth 8 and six
two-qubit gates.

## Constraints

- `qubits` is exactly 3 and `clbits` is exactly 0.
- At most six two-qubit gates, which is three per bond.
- Allowed gates: `x` carrying a single control, `crx`, `p`, `rz`, `rx`, `h`.
- No `rxx`, `ryy`, `rzz` or `swap`: writing the answer as three two-qubit rotations per bond is the construction the budget exists to exclude.
- No `measure`, no `reset`, no classical conditions.

## Scoring

300 points for the first accepted submission. The judge runs `unitary_equals`
against $U$ with `up_to_global_phase` set to true and a tolerance of $10^{-9}$,
because the fractional SWAP identity carries a factor of $e^{-i\theta/2}$ per
bond, then `resource_bound` with `max_two_qubit_gates` of 6, then
`gate_set_subset` over the list above. No partial credit, one penalty minute per
rejected submission.

## Qiskit implementation

Your `solve` is in scope. This is the exact translation the judge uses:

```python
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.circuit.library import standard_gates as sg
from qiskit.quantum_info import Operator

NAMES = {"crx": sg.CRXGate, "p": sg.PhaseGate, "x": sg.XGate}

def build(ir):
    """RedQuant IR -> Qiskit. Wire q becomes Qiskit wire n-1-q, because qubit 0
    is our most significant bit and Qiskit's least. With that one change the
    amplitude arrays line up index for index."""
    n = ir["qubits"]
    w = lambda q: n - 1 - q
    qc = QuantumCircuit(n)
    for op in ir["ops"]:
        g = NAMES[op["gate"]](*op.get("params", []))
        controls = op.get("controls", [])
        if controls:
            g = g.control(len(controls))
        qc.append(g, [w(q) for q in controls + op["targets"]])
    return qc

u = Operator(build(solve())).data
out = u[:, 0b100]
for i, amp in enumerate(out):
    if abs(amp) > 1e-9:
        print(f"|{i:03b}>", complex(round(amp.real, 6) + 0.0, round(amp.imag, 6) + 0.0),
              "probability", round(abs(amp) ** 2, 6))
```

Output:

```text
|001> (-0.125-0.216506j) probability 0.0625
|010> (0.375-0.216506j) probability 0.1875
|100> (0.75+0.433013j) probability 0.75
```
