## The intended solution

CCZ is diagonal, so the whole problem lives in the exponent. Write the phase it
applies to $\ket{x_0 x_1 x_2}$ as $\exp(i\pi x_0 x_1 x_2)$ and expand the cubic
product in terms of parities, which is what a circuit of CNOTs and $T$ gates can
actually produce. The identity is

$$
x_0 x_1 x_2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\Big[ x_0 + x_1 + x_2 - (x_0 \oplus x_1) - (x_1 \oplus x_2) - (x_0 \oplus x_2) + (x_0 \oplus x_1 \oplus x_2) \Big],
$$

valid for bits, and the factor of $1/4$ is exactly why $T = \mathrm{diag}(1, e^{i\pi/4})$
is the right unit of currency: seven terms, each worth one $\pi/4$ rotation,
signed as the identity says.

The circuit realises it by parking each parity on a wire long enough to rotate
it. Alternating CNOTs from qubit 1 and qubit 0 into qubit 2 make qubit 2 hold, in
turn, $x_2$, then $x_1 \oplus x_2$, then $x_0 \oplus x_1 \oplus x_2$, then
$x_0 \oplus x_2$, with a $T$ or $T^{\dagger}$ applied at each stop. The tail,
`t` on qubit 0, `tdg` on qubit 1, and a CNOT pair on those two wires, supplies
the remaining $x_0$, $x_1$ and $x_0 \oplus x_1$ terms. Seven $T$ gates, six
CNOTs, depth 10, and the resulting operator has diagonal
`[1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, -1]` with a largest off-diagonal entry of $0.0$.

Seven is not an accident of this particular circuit. Seven is the known minimum
T-count for an exact ancilla-free Clifford plus $T$ implementation of CCZ or the
Toffoli. You can beat it only by changing the rules: with one ancilla and a
mid-circuit measurement with classical feedforward, the count drops to four.
Neither is allowed here.

## Why the naive answer is too deep

Two naive answers show up, and they fail in opposite directions.

The first is to write `ccz` and hope. The gate-set check rejects it, because the
point of the problem is that a fault-tolerant machine does not have a CCZ
instruction to call.

The second is to reach for a generic synthesis routine: decompose the diagonal
$8 \times 8$ unitary with a standard multiplexed-rotation cascade, or run a
Toffoli through a naive controlled-controlled-$U$ construction that expands
$C\text{-}V$, $C\text{-}V^{\dagger}$ and $C\text{-}V$ with $V = \sqrt{X}$. That
route is correct and costs far more: the $\sqrt{X}$ construction alone needs
three controlled-$V$ gates, each of which is two CNOTs plus $T$-family
rotations once you push it into Clifford plus $T$, and the T-count lands in the
teens rather than at seven.

## Common wrong answers

Truncating the tail is the classic. Stopping after the first nine ops leaves a
perfectly legal diagonal unitary,

```text
[1, 1, e^(i pi/4), e^(i pi/4), 1, 1, e^(-i pi/4), e^(3 i pi/4)]
```

which is not CCZ and is not even $-1$ on $\ket{111}$. `unitary_equals` with
`up_to_global_phase` rejects it, because a global phase multiplies all eight
entries by the same number and these entries disagree with each other.

Swapping any single `t` for a `tdg`, or the reverse, has a sharper signature:
whichever of the seven you flip, exactly four of the eight diagonal entries come
out wrong and the rest are untouched. The failure message names the first
mismatch, which tells you which parity term you rotated the wrong way.
