## The intended solution

Fan out by doubling. One Hadamard, then a CNOT that turns one entangled qubit
into two, then two CNOTs in parallel that turn two into four.

```text
layer 0:  H  on qubit 0
layer 1:  CNOT 0 -> 1
layer 2:  CNOT 0 -> 2   and   CNOT 1 -> 3
```

The two gates in layer 2 touch wires 0, 2 and 1, 3, which are disjoint, so the
ASAP scheduler puts them in the same layer. Depth 3, three CNOTs, and the state
comes out as $0.707107$ on $\ket{0000}$ and $0.707107$ on $\ket{1111}$.

Why it works: after layer 1 the register is
$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{00} + \ket{11})$ on wires 0 and 1. A CNOT from an
already-correlated wire onto a fresh $\ket{0}$ extends the correlation without
disturbing it, because $\mathrm{CNOT}\ket{a}\ket{0} = \ket{a}\ket{a}$ on basis
states and the map is linear. Two wires can each do that at once, so the number
of entangled qubits doubles per layer. For $n$ qubits the depth is
$1 + \lceil \log_2 n \rceil$.

## Why the naive answer is too deep

The chain, `h` on qubit 0 then CNOT 0 to 1, 1 to 2, 2 to 3, is correct and has
depth 4. Each CNOT reuses the wire the previous one just wrote, so no two of
them can share a layer. It fails `max_depth` of 3 by exactly one layer, and the
gap grows linearly: at $n = 16$ the chain costs depth 16 and the doubling costs
depth 5.

On real hardware the difference is larger than the layer count suggests, because
the chain also holds the earliest qubit idle and decohering for the whole run.
The doubling circuit finishes every wire's work within a couple of layers of
when that wire is first touched.

## Common wrong answers

Trying to parallelise the chain by writing CNOT 0 to 1 and CNOT 1 to 2 next to
each other does not help: they share wire 1, so the scheduler separates them
anyway, and depth is still 4. The ASAP rule is derived from the ops you submit,
not from the order you write them in, so reordering the list changes nothing
unless the dependency structure changes.

Using three Hadamards and three CZ gates instead reaches
$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\ket{0000} + \ket{1111})$ only if you also undo the extra
Hadamards, and the undo costs another layer. A CZ sandwiched between two
Hadamards on the target is a CNOT, which is the identity you would use if the
hardware had no CNOT, but it does not save depth here.
