## The intended solution

Start with the arithmetic, not the circuit. With $N = 8$ and $M = 2$,

$$
\theta = 2\arcsin\sqrt{M/N} = 2\arcsin\tfrac12 = \frac{\pi}{3},
$$

and after $k$ iterations the probability on the marked subspace is
$\sin^2\!\big((2k+1)\theta/2\big) = \sin^2\!\big((2k+1)\pi/6\big)$. That is
$0.25$ at $k = 0$, exactly $1$ at $k = 1$, and back to $0.25$ at $k = 2$. One
iteration, and it is exact rather than approximate, because $\theta$ divides
$\pi/2$ a whole number of times only for these small cases.

The circuit is then routine:

- Hadamard all three wires.
- Oracle: flip the sign of $\ket{010}$ and of $\ket{101}$. Each is a `ccz`
  conjugated by $X$ gates on the wires that read 0 in that string. For $010$ that
  is $X$ on wires 0 and 2; for $101$ it is $X$ on wire 1.
- Diffusion: $H^{\otimes 3}$, $X^{\otimes 3}$, `ccz`, $X^{\otimes 3}$,
  $H^{\otimes 3}$, which is the reflection about the uniform state.

Three three-wire gates in total, 24 ops, depth 11. Running it gives amplitude
$-0.707107$ on each of $\ket{010}$ and $\ket{101}$ and zero everywhere else. The
overall minus sign is a global phase and is accepted.

Marking two states costs two `ccz` gates but no extra iterations. That is the
part worth internalising: doubling $M$ shortens the search, it does not lengthen
the oracle in any way that matters.

## Why the naive answer is wrong, not just slow

The instinct is to compute the iteration count from
$\lfloor \tfrac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{N/M} \rfloor = \lfloor 1.5708 \rfloor = 1$, which
happens to give the right answer here, and then to assume the result is
approximate and add a second iteration for safety. A second iteration is not
insurance. It rotates past the target and lands back at $0.25$, throwing away
three quarters of the probability you already had. Grover is a rotation, not a
convergent process.

The other naive move is to build the oracle from a full multi-controlled
construction with an ancilla: allocate a fourth wire, compute the AND of the
three query bits into it with two Toffolis, phase it, uncompute. That is correct
and costs four wires and four three-wire gates for what an $X$-conjugated `ccz`
does in one, so it fails `max_qubits` and the multi-qubit gate check at the same
time.

## Common wrong answers

Conjugating with $X$ on the wrong wires marks the complement string. Marking
$\ket{101}$ requires an $X$ on wire 1 only, because wires 0 and 2 already read 1.
Putting the $X$ gates on wires 0 and 2 instead makes the second block mark
$\ket{010}$ as well, the same state the first block marked, and two sign flips of
one state cancel. The oracle then marks nothing, the diffusion operator has
nothing to amplify, and the circuit returns the flat distribution: every one of
the eight outcomes at $0.125$.

Omitting the $X^{\otimes 3}$ layers inside the diffusion operator reflects about
$\ket{111}$ rather than about the uniform superposition. It is a legitimate
unitary and it is not Grover's diffusion, and it produces the same tell: all
eight outcomes at $0.125$. A flat histogram is the signature of a broken Grover
circuit, and it is worth checking for before you start rereading your angles.
